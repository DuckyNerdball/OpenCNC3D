# Rally

Rally is a kart race with the game's own vehicles, played on purpose-built course maps.
It sits beside Free for All and Gun Game as a game mode, chosen with the Game Mode
dropdown in the host's lobby. Every racer picks a vehicle, lines up on the start grid, and races laps of a
circuit through enemy defences: the start line is the finish line, and a race is one to
nine laps (three unless the lobby says otherwise). The first to complete the laps wins;
everyone else is ranked behind. After the results the deciding player races again on the
same course or ends the match.

This file is the design and the contract between the three parts that make it:

- the **brain module** `brain/vanilla/tiberiandawn/rally.cpp` (+ `rally.h`), which owns
  the race: vehicle physics, the course, crates and items, damage and respawn, the AI
  drivers and the finishing order. It runs on engine ticks in integer arithmetic from
  lockstep events, so every LAN peer computes the same race;
- the **eyes module** `game/rally_mod.h`, included by `game/cnc_eyes.cpp`, which sends the
  local player's controls, draws the player's own vehicle where those controls are taking
  it (so a network race answers the keys at once, see "No control lag in a network race"),
  follows it with the camera, draws the HUD and the crosshair, and applies the vehicle's
  height, pitch, roll and heading to its model;
- the **course maps** SCM70EA (Tiberium Run), SCM71EA (Nod Gauntlet) and SCM72EA
  (Frostbite Pass), three circuits generated by `tools/rally/make_course.py`, checked by
  `tools/rally/check_course.py` and staged into `game/missions/` like any other
  multiplayer map;
- the **menus** (`app/opencnc3d.cpp`, `menu/`), where Rally under the lobby's Game Mode
  dropdown lists only courses and Match Rules sets the laps.

`game/rally_abi.h` is the data contract between brain and eyes (state structs, flags,
item ids, control bits, exported function names). Both sides include it.

## Rules of the race

- 2 to 8 racers: the match's players. Human seats drive with the keyboard and mouse;
  AI seats are driven by the brain's AI drivers. A skirmish is the player and AI racers.
- **Phases.** PICK (every player chooses a vehicle, 20 s, unpicked seats get a random
  one) -> COUNTDOWN (3, 2, 1, GO on the grid, 4 s) -> RACE -> DONE (the results) -> PICK
  again on RACE AGAIN, or OVER on END.
- **Laps.** A course with `Loop=1` is a circuit: the start line is the finish line and a
  race is N laps, N from the lobby (1 to 9, default 3; `RALLY_LAPS_*` in
  `game/rally_abi.h`, set with `OpenCNC3D_Rally_Configure` before the match loads, and
  the host's number travels to every joiner). A lap counts when the racer crosses the
  line moving forward (the direction from P000 to P001) after passing at least 90% of the
  lap's points since the last count; a safety net counts it two cells past the end of the
  lap if the line was somehow missed. A course without `Loop` is point to point and is
  one run whatever the lobby says. `progress` runs 0..65536 over the WHOLE race, so the
  results' "87%" means 87% of the race; `lap_progress` is within the lap being driven.
- **Finish.** Completing the last lap finishes. The first finisher wins. The race ends
  when every player has finished (computer drivers still out are ranked by progress);
  30 s after the winner when only computer drivers are still out; 60 s after the winner
  when a player is still out, unless that player is on the last lap with 90% of it
  behind them; and in any case 3 minutes per lap after GO.
- **Race again or end.** DONE shows the results and waits for the deciding player
  (`RallyState.decider`): the lowest-seat human still in the match, which is the host of
  a network match and the player in a skirmish. RACE AGAIN goes back to PICK on
  the same course and laps with the course put back as it started: every building of the
  hostile house repaired or rebuilt at its starting strength and facing, its units and
  soldiers put back where they started, fallen trees standing again, shells in flight and
  hazards gone, crates back, and every racer on the grid. Each seat's last vehicle is
  preselected and raced again if the seat picks nothing. END ends the match
  with the winner's house victorious. In a skirmish Enter is RACE AGAIN and Escape is END;
  in a network match both keys stay the game's (Return opens the chat line, Escape the
  menu), so the deciding player can still talk on the results, and the choice is the two
  buttons'. Everyone else reads whom they are waiting for. With
  no human left in the match it ends by itself 10 s after the results. The choice is a
  lockstep order, acted on only from the decider and only once, so every peer makes the
  same one on the same tick.
- **Seats that change hands.** A seat handed to the computer during the race is driven
  by an AI driver from then on. A seat whose house is defeated retires: it is never put
  back on the course, is ranked last and cannot win.
- **Health** is the vehicle's engine strength. A destroyed racer explodes, waits 3 s and
  respawns where a rescue would put it (below) with full health and 2 s of damage
  immunity.
- **Flipping.** Explosions, mines and heavy rams can throw a vehicle into the air with
  spin. Landing past 90 degrees of roll or pitch leaves it flipped. After 3 s flipped it
  rights itself automatically (a short hop and roll back upright) and drives on.
- **Rescue.** R puts the racer back on the course at its last checkpoint (3 s cooldown),
  for when it is wedged or lost. Only a press in the race counts: one made on the grid
  during the countdown is dropped, not kept for GO. A racer that has not yet crossed the
  start line goes back to its own grid slot (point 0 is the line itself, ahead of the
  whole grid). Its distance along the course is then counted from the point it was put
  back at, never from whichever course segment happens to be nearest (on a circuit's line
  the lap's last segment is as near as its first), and a lap's checkpoints are read off
  that distance, so neither a rescue nor a respawn can gain ground or count a lap.
- **Places** are live: laps done, then the distance along the course. On the grid, before
  GO, that distance is negative and orders the rows, so the countdown already shows the
  front row first.
- **Enemy defences** are structures and units on the map owned by the course's hostile
  house (guard towers, gun turrets, obelisks, advanced guard towers, base buildings).
  They attack every racer with the engine's normal targeting, and racers can destroy them.
  Some engine rules change in a race only, because a racer is far faster than the units
  they were written for:
  - an obelisk charges in about 1.3 s instead of four, and keeps charging and stays
    charged while a racer is within its range plus 2 cells;
  - shells fired at a racer are aimed where it will be when they land, and an explosion
    counts from the edge of the vehicle rather than its centre;
  - no single hit from a defence takes more than 85% of a racer's full health, so a
    racer at full health survives one obelisk blast;
  - the hovercraft, which the engine never lets anything target, is a legal target for
    the defences and the other racers like any racer;
  - walls take no damage, since they are the edges of the track and a shot-away sandbag
    would open a gap in it.
- **Crates** are only the course's own: the engine's random multiplayer crates never
  appear in a race.
- **Results.** The results table lists every racer in finishing order, then the rest by
  how far along the course they got. A finisher shows its time, a racer still on the
  course when the race ends shows its progress (87%), and a seat that left the match
  shows OUT.

## Vehicles

All ground vehicles of Tiberian Dawn: Mammoth Tank, Medium Tank, Light Tank, Stealth
Tank, Flame Tank, APC, Rocket Launcher (MLRS), Hum-vee, Nod Buggy, Recon Bike,
Harvester, Artillery, SSM Launcher, Hovercraft, Mobile HQ and MCV. The gunboat
(water only), the visceroid and the dinosaurs are not vehicles a player drives.

Each has kart stats in the brain's table: top speed, acceleration, turn rate, grip,
mass and collision radius. Light vehicles are fast and fragile, heavy ones slow,
tough and push others around. Health is the engine strength. The hovercraft slides
more and can cross water. Vehicles with a weapon fire it; those without (Harvester,
MCV, Mobile HQ, Hovercraft) rely on items and ramming.

## Controls

| Input | Action |
| --- | --- |
| W / Up | accelerate |
| S / Down | brake, then reverse |
| A, D / Left, Right | steer |
| Shift (hold) | drift; releasing after a long drift gives a mini-turbo |
| Mouse | aims the crosshair; a turret turns toward it |
| Left button | fire the vehicle's weapon at the crosshair |

A crosshair within 0.75 cell of a rival fires at that rival. A weapon without a turret
fires only within 30 degrees of the vehicle's nose; aim outside that cone is turned to
its edge.
| Space or right button | use the held item |
| R | rescue to the last checkpoint |

## Physics

Integer only, deterministic on every peer: no floating point and no C library maths in
the simulation; sine comes from `rally_trig.h`.

- Position in fine units (1/256 lepton, 65536 per cell), heading as a 16-bit angle
  (0 north, clockwise; `yaw >> 8` is the engine DirType).
- Several substeps per engine tick. Constants are written per second and converted
  with the match's engine ticks per second, so the race runs at the same real speed
  at every game speed setting.
- Velocity is split into forward and sideways parts. Throttle accelerates toward the
  top speed (scaled by the ground: clear and road full, rough and beach slower,
  Tiberium slower still). Sideways speed is removed by grip; drifting and being in
  the air remove much less. Steering turns the heading at a rate that grows with speed
  and reverses when reversing.
- Drift starts with Shift and a steer key and keeps turning that way while Shift is
  held: steering into it turns at 1.2 times the normal rate, steering out of it at 0.6
  times. Half a second of drift charges the mini-turbo, and letting go of Shift fires it.
- The top speeds sit in a narrow band (the slowest vehicle has 0.89 of the fastest's),
  and a vehicle without a weapon is a little faster than an armed one of its weight, so
  every vehicle can win.
- Collision: the vehicle is a circle. Impassable cells for its locomotion (walls,
  buildings, trees, rock, cliffs, water unless hovering, the map edge) are squares;
  the circle is pushed out and bounces with restitution, and a hard hit costs health.
  Two racers bounce off each other by mass; a hard ram damages the lighter.
  Infantry is run over.
- Height: a racer can be in the air (z, vertical speed, gravity, pitch and roll spin).

## Items

Crates sit in rows across the course and come back 5 s after they are taken. Driving
through one spins the item slot for a second and gives an item, weighted by race
position the way kart racers do it: the leader gets defensive items, the back of the
pack the strong ones. One item is held at a time.

| Item | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Nitro | a speed burst |
| Triple Nitro | three bursts |
| Mine | dropped behind; whoever drives over it is thrown and spun |
| Rocket | fired straight ahead, bounces off walls twice |
| Homing Missile | follows the course to the racer one place ahead, then seeks it once it is close and in sight |
| Ion Cannon | after a warning the Ion Cannon strikes the race leader |
| Armour | 6 s invulnerable and faster; rammed racers are thrown |
| Cloak | 6 s untargetable by defences, racers and homing missiles |
| Repair | full health |

The homing missile and the Ion Cannon pass over racers who have finished; with no racer
to strike, the item is kept rather than spent. An AI driver uses Repair below 75%
health, after holding it 8 s with any damage, or after 15 s.

## Course map format

A course is a classic Tiberian Dawn multiplayer map, `SCMnnEA.INI` + `.BIN` in
`game/missions/`: 64 x 64 cells (cell = y*64 + x) with the playable area set by
`[MAP] X, Y, Width, Height` (1, 1, 62, 62 on the three courses), plus a `[Rally]`
section. Courses use SCM70 to SCM79; SCM70EA, SCM71EA and SCM72EA exist. Every cell of
the .BIN is clear ground, so the courses are flat and the vehicle physics and the drawn
ground cannot disagree about a slope. A `[Rally]` section is what makes a map a course:
the game races it, and the lobby lists it under Rally only.

```
[Rally]
Name=Tiberium Run
Hostile=BadGuy            ; house whose map structures and units attack every racer
Loop=1                    ; a circuit; absent or 0: point to point
Points=N                  ; course centre line, at most 512
P000=x,y,hw               ; leptons (cell*256 + offset), hw = driveable half width in leptons
...
GridFacing=dir            ; engine DirType 0..255 the grid faces
Grid0=x,y ... Grid7=x,y   ; leptons; Grid0 is pole position
Finish=x1,y1,x2,y2        ; finish line segment in leptons
Crates=M
C00=x,y                   ; leptons, crate spots
```

**On a circuit** (`Loop=1`) the points go once round the lap: P000 is ON the start/finish
line, P001 onward follow the direction of travel, and the last point is about one cell
short of P000 (P000 is not repeated; the closing segment back to it is part of the lap).
`Finish` is the start/finish line itself: across the track at P000, square to the
direction of travel, P000's half width either side, from the left of the direction of
travel to the right. It is exactly the segment the eyes draw as the start line, so the
start strip and the finish strip coincide. Crossing it forwards after going round
completes a lap. The grid stands behind the line on the lap's final straight, facing the
direction of travel: two columns 0.75 cell either side of the centre line, rows 0.75,
2.25, 3.75 and 5.25 cells behind P000.

**On a point-to-point course** (no `Loop`) the points run from the start to the finish,
the grid rows sit 1.25 to 5.75 cells behind the start, `Finish` is a segment across the
far end, and the race is one run. The format is unchanged from the first version and the
brain still races it; no shipped course is one any more.

Each half width is measured, on both kinds of course: the distance from the point to the
nearest blocked cell's square, less 16 leptons. No course has a fork; the `Branches`/`B0*`
keys of the first point-to-point courses are gone and the brain never read them.

The track's edges are sandbag wall overlay (SBAG) in `[OVERLAY]`; the defences are
`[STRUCTURES]` (and optionally `[UNITS]`/`[INFANTRY]`) owned by the hostile house. Each
racer's `[MultiN]` section allies it only with itself.

`tools/rally/make_course.py` writes a course from a description in the script
(`"loop": True` for a circuit, whose two "fit" straights it solves so the lap closes
exactly). `tools/rally/check_course.py SCM70EA SCM71EA SCM72EA` checks every course: the
lap closes, P000's finish line and the grid behind it, every half width against the
walls, a lap of 150 to 230 cells on a circuit (250 to 350 on a point-to-point course),
and the lane rule: parts of the course more than 12 points apart along the lap are at
least 4 cells apart, so two walls and at least one cell of ground separate them and no
wall is shared.

**The three circuits.**

| Course | Theater | Direction | Lap | Defences | Crates |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| SCM70EA Tiberium Run | TEMPERATE | anticlockwise | 184 points, 183.6 cells | 3 gun turrets, 2 guard towers | 14 spots in 4 rows |
| SCM71EA Nod Gauntlet | DESERT | clockwise | 182 points, 181.9 cells | 4 gun turrets, 1 obelisk | 13 spots in 4 rows |
| SCM72EA Frostbite Pass | WINTER | clockwise | 172 points, 171.9 cells | 6 advanced guard towers | 11 spots in 3 rows |

- **Tiberium Run**: the start/finish straight runs east along the south edge between
  Tiberium fields; a chicane up the east side narrows to four cells between two guard
  towers; S-bends run west along the north edge past the village, then a long run down
  the west side. The Nod base and Tiberium fields sit in the infield. Its four corners are
  radius 6, tight enough that a drift pays.
- **Nod Gauntlet**: the start/finish straight runs east along the north edge; down the
  east side, then west through the middle of the Nod base, narrowing between two
  turrets with buildings close on both sides; south past the outworks, west under one
  obelisk two cells back from the straight, and up the west side through S-bends. The
  hardest course: the obelisk alone destroys several racers a race.
- **Frostbite Pass**: two sweeping arcs round one centre, 12 cells apart, joined by two
  tight U-turns; the pass between them narrows between two advanced guard towers. The
  start is on the west straight, so the first bend off the grid is the long sweep, not a
  hairpin.

Measured with the probe (4 racers, best computer driver): a flying lap of 30.7 s, 32.4 s
and 29.0 s, and a 3-lap race won in about 92, 97 and 86 s.

## Starting a race

**Choosing Rally.** The Skirmish lobby and the host's network room have a Game Mode
dropdown at the right end of their tab row: Free for All (where every lobby opens), Gun
Game and Rally (`sk_set_mode` in `menu/doslobby.c`, drawn by `modebutton` / `modelist` in
`menu/enhanced_menu.cpp`). Rally is Tiberian Dawn's; Red Alert shows it greyed, "Dawn3D
only for now", as it does Gun Game. A joiner sees the host's mode, greyed; the dropdown is
locked while a room's start is agreed. Changing it refilters the map list at once and, in
a room, reaches every joiner on the next push and clears their ready. A map whose INI has
a `[Rally]` section is a course, and a course is always raced when it is played as a
skirmish or a network match (`rly_will_race` in `game/rally_mod.h`), whatever mode led
there. The mode filters: under Rally the skirmish lobby, the network room and the host's map picker
list only courses; under Free for All and Gun Game courses are hidden. One predicate,
`sk_map_fits_mode` in `menu/doslobby.c`, decides every list, the default map and a
remembered map. A joiner shows the host's map whatever its own filter. The app's skirmish
scan marks each course (`SK_Map.course`: a point-to-point run or a circuit, its log line
ending `|rally course` or `|rally circuit`).

**Match Rules under Rally** shows THE RACE, a few lines saying what a race is and its
keys, and on the right Laps (1 to 9, default 3; greyed on a point-to-point course) and,
in a network room, AI Takeover, with Game Speed as before. Tech level, credits, units,
bases, Tiberium growth, superweapons, crates and short game are not offered, since a
race reads none of them, and teams are locked: every racer is on its own. In a seat's
own settings Team and Start position are not offered either (the race draws its grid at
random); the side is, because it picks the vehicle's livery. The laps are
remembered with the skirmish rules (`opencnc3d-skirmish.cfg`, version 3, the last number
of the rules line). The room's label in the LAN advert, the room list and the internet
list's row reads `<map> / Rally`. A room a player hosts opens in Free for All, after
a match too: the room kept after a match is the one just played, which may have been
another mode's room joined from the list, so a kept room on a course goes back to the
first ordinary map. A
course is never also a Gun Game, whatever a room says (`g_gunOnCourse` in
`game/cnc_eyes.cpp`).

**The laps on the wire.** A network room's laps travel host to joiners in the room
setup, `NmSetup.rally_laps` at byte 31 (`NM_VERSION` 23), zero for every other kind of
room; a nonzero value is also how a joiner's screens know the room is a Rally before its
map is read. Every peer calls `OpenCNC3D_Rally_Configure` with that number before the
match loads (`rly_arm`: the room's number in a network match, else `--laps`, else 3), so
every peer races the same laps.

**On a network** every peer reads the same course, so the peers agree it is a race without
the match setup carrying a switch: a peer with different map files is refused by the
scenario hash, and the race is folded into the order-wire layout word both ends compare on
the command-line path, so a build from before Rally is refused there rather than playing
the course as an ordinary skirmish. ABI 2 added a lockstep order (`RALLY_CHOICE`, after
Gun Game's orders), which changes the event table's hash, so a build with the first Rally
brain and one with this brain refuse each other in the lobby. While a race runs, the
race's own hash is folded into the desync alarm's per-frame hash. The headless test aids
(`rallyauto`, `rallydebug`) are refused in a network match.

**From the command line**, `--skirmish` on a course map starts a race against the AI
seats, `--laps N` sets the laps (1 to 9), and `--rally` is accepted and asks for nothing
more (`--rally` on a course without `--skirmish` is refused). For the app: `--rally`
chooses Rally in the lobby's Game Mode dropdown on `--lobbyplay` (opening the list and
pressing its row) and opens a Rally room on `--mpplay`, and `--mphost` on a course
(`--mpmap SCM70EA`) opens a Rally room with `--laps` when given.

`tools/rally/write-test-starters.py` writes one double-click starter per course into a
play folder (`START-RALLY-<n>-<course>.command`) or a staged Windows package (`.bat`),
each a race of three laps against seven computer drivers, and a `RALLY-READ-ME.txt` that
says how to start a network race from the lobby.

## HUD

The sidebar is hidden; the battlefield takes the whole window. The race is drawn in the
Enhanced graphics mode and watched through the N64 camera at its start zoom, whatever
the player had chosen; both are handed back when the match ends, and a setting the
player changes during the race (mouse buttons, display, frame counter) is saved then.
The game speed is held at the speed the race started at. The camera follows the
player's vehicle, leading a little in the direction of travel.

The HUD uses one whole-number zoom for all of its art and text, from the window size
(the 1995 six-point font needs about twice its size to read), never below the game's
own HUD zoom and divided like it by the UI Scale setting. A panel that would not fit at
that zoom takes the largest one at which it does.

- top left: race position (1st..8th), race time, on a circuit LAP n/N (gold on the last
  lap), health bar;
- top centre: the item slot (spinning while a crate's item is chosen);
- left edge: a progress strip over the whole race, with one marker per racer in its
  house colour and a mark between laps;
- centre: 3, 2, 1, GO; WRONG WAY; the flip-back countdown;
- the vehicle chooser during PICK: one original cameo per vehicle with speed, handling
  and armour bars; on a race again the last race's vehicle is highlighted and shown with
  "ENTER KEEPS IT", and Enter sends that pick (a vehicle not chosen by the end of PICK is
  kept anyway);
- the results table in DONE: position, colour, name, the vehicle's cameo (its name
  instead when a cameo per row would not fit, as with eight racers in a 720-row
  window), and the time, the progress or OUT. The title reads "RACE 2 RESULTS" from the
  second race. Under the table the deciding player has RACE AGAIN and END buttons; in a
  skirmish Enter and Escape do the same (handled before the game's own) and a line names
  the keys, and in a network match the keys stay the game's and the line reads "THE
  OTHERS WAIT FOR YOUR CHOICE". Everybody else reads "WAITING FOR <name>". Once a choice is made
  the line reads "RACING AGAIN ON THIS COURSE" or "THE MATCH IS ENDING"; with no decider
  it counts down the ten seconds, "THE MATCH ENDS IN n".

The mouse pointer is a small crosshair while racing. New icons (items) are pixel art in
the style of the game's own cameos and sidebar art.

## No control lag in a network race

A network match is lockstep: every order is stamped a number of turns ahead (the room's
lookahead, 3 to 12, `LS_AHEAD` in `net/lockstep.h`) and runs on that frame at every peer,
which is what keeps the peers' worlds identical. Drawn as executed, a racer would answer
its own keys 3 to 12 ticks late, 170 to 660 ms at a race's 18 ticks a second; no racing
game asks that of a player. Rally removes the lag by client-side prediction, for this
mode only. The lockstep, the order wire, the simulation and every other mode are
unchanged.

- **What is predicted.** Only the local racer, as drawn on this machine: its position,
  heading, height, pitch and roll, its turret, the camera that follows it, the crosshair's
  off-ground fallback and its drift, boost and airborne look. Every other racer, every
  defence and every shot is drawn as executed.
- **How.** The eyes keep every control they sent (`RlyCtl`: the keys, the aim, and the
  engine frame it runs on). In a network match that frame is stamped when the network
  drains the brain's order queue (`rly_on_drain`: the brain's frame plus the room's
  lookahead, `nm_ahead`); in a skirmish it is the next tick. Every drawn frame, the eyes
  build one step per engine tick from the newest executed tick to the frame a control
  sent now would run on, each holding the newest control that runs by then, and ask the
  brain where that leaves the racer (`OpenCNC3D_Rally_Predict`). The brain runs its own
  driving code on a copy of the racer, so the live race and the prediction share one code
  path, and it is pure: it changes nothing and draws no random number, so calling it any
  number of times cannot desync a match. The racer is drawn between the predictions for
  n - 1 and n steps at the frame's fraction of a tick. A key pressed now is sent now and
  is in those steps at once, so the drawn racer answers on the next frame.
- **What it leaves out** (the next executed tick corrects it): the other racers and
  contact with them, hits, throws and spins, crates and the item roulette, every item
  but nitro, the rescue key, wall damage (the wall bounce itself is predicted), and
  crossing the finish line. While the racer is dead, flipped, righting, spinning,
  finished or driven by an AI driver, it is drawn as executed.
- **The correction.** When a new executed tick arrives, what the previous frame predicted
  for its last frame is compared with the same steps run from the new tick, so the
  difference measures only what other machines did. It is drawn away with a 50 ms time
  constant (95% gone in 150 ms) rather than snapped. It is a cut instead when it is larger
  than the racer could have driven in the ticks it is drawn ahead (those ticks at half as
  fast again as its top speed, plus a cell, and never under 3 cells: `rly_snap_cells`),
  or a quarter turn, on a new vehicle or a respawn, and when the executed racer jumps 2
  cells (a rescue). A fixed 3-cell limit made every finish, spin or hit at a long
  lookahead, where the drawn racer runs up to about 5 cells ahead, a jump back. A control sent just before a tick re-predicts the cached frame first,
  so this machine's own key is never mistaken for a correction. The prediction starting
  or stopping is blended from the last drawn pose the same way. While a turn is late (this
  tick's orders are sent but a peer's have not arrived, so the world waits), the racer is
  held where the last frame before the wait drew it, one step short of the horizon the
  sent orders moved on, so a late turn is a pause like everyone else's and not a leap
  forward and back. When a race starts again
  the brain seats every racer with no keys held, so the eyes forget what they sent and
  send the keys held now again.
- **Switch for comparison.** `OPENCNC3D_RALLY_PREDICT=0`, or the script verb
  `rallypredict off`, draws the racer as executed. `OPENCNC3D_NETTEST_AHEAD=N` seats a
  network room at lookahead N (clamped 3..12) to measure a far link on one machine;
  nothing in play sets it.

Measured headless on loopback (4 drawn frames a tick, 18 ticks a second, so one frame is
13.9 ms; frames from a steering key to the drawn heading moving):

| Match | Drawn as executed | Predicted | Executed racer |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Skirmish | 2-3 frames (28-42 ms) | 0 | 1-2 |
| Network, lookahead 3 | 14-16 frames (194-222 ms) | 0 | 13-15 |
| Network, lookahead 12 | 50-53 frames (694-736 ms) | 0-1 | 49-51 |

Prediction on and off give the same race hash on every peer at every tick compared:
drawing never touches the simulation. In a six-racer network race most corrections are
under 0.04 cells; the largest measured were about 0.5 to 1.1 cells, at contact with a
rival, blended out in 150 ms.

## Testing a race headless

- `brain/host/rally_probe.cpp` drives the brain alone, with no renderer: `rally_probe
  <dylib> <scen_dir/> <SCEN> content/ <log> [seed] [max_seconds] [laps] [races]`, run
  from a folder holding `CONQUER.MIX` and `content/`. It races every seat (seat 0 is its
  own test driver, partly hand driven), checks the rules (laps, places, finish, defences,
  drift and mini-turbo, race again and the course reset, prediction against the executed
  race), and runs every race twice, the second time calling Predict heavily, and requires
  the two to hash the same on every tick. Only that run's calls for seat 0 while it is
  driven by hand actually run the driving physics (measured over 80 three-lap runs:
  900 to 2,100 of 23,000 to 55,000 calls a run, which the report prints); the rest answer 0 before simulating
  anything. It also holds a racer to never being put back ahead of where it was: a lap
  or the finish counts only after the racer has come near three quarters of the course's
  points since the last one, and race 2 presses the rescue key in the countdown (dropped)
  and at GO (back to the grid slot, behind the line), destroys a computer driver on the
  grid (back behind the line, on lap 1), and puts seat 0 back exactly on the line just
  after a new lap and drives on (no second lap). In the countdown the places must be the
  grid's rows. `brain/host/rally_test/` holds two small test
  courses: RALLYT01 point to point and RALLYT02 a loop (`make_loop_course.py`).
- Script verbs for `--script` runs: `rallystate`, `rallylaps`, `rallypick N`,
  `rallyuntil PHASE MAXTICKS` (OVER counts the match ending), `rallykeys KEYS TICKS`
  (w s a d, h drift, f fire, i item, r rescue), `rallyaim X Y`, `rallychoice retry|end`,
  `rallypredict on|off`, `rallydrive KEYS TICKS FRAMES [EXTRA]` (holds the keys the way
  the live loop does, drawing FRAMES frames a tick, and logs every frame's executed and
  drawn racer as `RALLYDRIVE|` lines, which is how the latency above was measured),
  `rallyfollow TICKS [FRAMES]` (drives this machine's racer round the course by its own
  keys toward the centre line, pressing rescue when stuck, until it finishes: how a
  network race gets its players round the laps, since the autopilot is refused there),
  `rallyauto on|off` and `rallydebug` (both refused in a network match), and
  `expectrally FIELD OP VALUE` (fields include lap, laps, loop, lapprogress, racenumber,
  decider, choice, lastvehicle and predicted).
- A network race on one machine: `opencnc3d ... --mphost 40 --mpmap SCM70EA --mpbots 4
  --laps 2 --script host.txt` and, a few seconds later, `opencnc3d ... --mpjoin 40
  --mpmap SCM70EA --script join.txt`. Compare the `hash=` of `rallystate` lines at equal
  ticks on both sides. A host script that quits the instant its match ends can leave
  before the joiner has run the same tick, and the joiner then reports that the host
  left; end the host's script with `wait 4000` the way the game holds its own verdict.
